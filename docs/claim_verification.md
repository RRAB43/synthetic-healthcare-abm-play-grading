# Public Claim Verification

**Research date:** October 26, 2026

## Purpose

This document separates verified public evidence from hypotheses used in the
synthetic ABM framework.

All plays remain hypothetical. Public information about an organization does
not prove that the organization is a prospect, customer, or sales opportunity
for Ambience Healthcare.

## Evidence standard

| Status                      | Meaning                                                                                               |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Verified                    | Supported by an official organization or vendor source                                                |
| Verified with qualification | Supported by an attributable secondary source, but the exact detail was not found in a primary source |
| Unverified                  | The available source does not establish the claim                                                     |
| Cut                         | The claim should not appear in the portfolio write-up                                                 |

## Claim 1: Jefferson Health uses Abridge

**Status:** Verified

Jefferson Health states that its ambient documentation tool is Abridge. Its
official September 2025 announcement says Abridge creates structured notes
within the EHR and is available to clinicians across the organization.

**Approved wording:**

> Jefferson Health publicly identifies Abridge as its ambient clinical
> documentation platform.

**Do not claim:**

- Jefferson is an Ambience customer.
- Jefferson is currently replacing Abridge.
- Jefferson has expressed dissatisfaction with Abridge.
- Ambience has an active sales opportunity at Jefferson.

**Source:**

- [Jefferson Health: AMA Joy in Medicine recognition](https://www.jeffersonhealth.org/about-us/news/2025/09/ama-joy-in-medicine-award)

## Claim 2: Houston Methodist’s ambient tools

### Physician and hospital program

**Status:** Verified

Houston Methodist reports that it launched a hospital ambient-listening
program with Ambience Healthcare. Its September 2025 article describes use by
emergency-department and inpatient clinicians and integration with Epic.

**Approved wording:**

> Houston Methodist publicly reports using Ambience Healthcare for a hospital
> ambient-documentation program involving emergency-department and inpatient
> clinicians.

**Source:**

- [Houston Methodist: Ambient Tech in Hospitals](https://www.houstonmethodist.org/leading-medicine-blog/articles/2025/sep/ambient-tech-in-hospitals-lightening-the-note-taking-load/)

### Nursing voice-charting tool

**Status:** Unverified vendor

Houston Methodist reported in September 2026 that nurses at its Cypress
location were piloting voice-driven charting. That article does not identify
the vendor producing the nursing documentation.

The physician and hospital Ambience partnership does not, by itself, prove
that the nursing pilot uses Ambience.

**Approved wording:**

> Houston Methodist reports that nurses at Cypress are piloting voice-driven
> charting, but the public source reviewed does not name the vendor.

**Do not claim:**

- Ambience produces Houston Methodist’s nursing notes.
- Epic produces the nursing notes.
- The nursing pilot is an enterprise-wide rollout.
- The nursing pilot and physician program use the same tool.

**Source:**

- [Houston Methodist: Intelligent Hospital of the Future](https://www.houstonmethodist.org/leading-medicine-blog/articles/2026/sep/how-houston-methodist-is-building-the-intelligent-hospital-of-the-future-through-virtual-care-ai-and/)

## Claim 3: AtlantiCare’s 26-specialty scope

**Status:** Verified with qualification

A July 2025 Becker’s Hospital Review article quotes AtlantiCare CIO Jordan
Ruch saying that approximately 260 providers across 26 specialties were using
Oracle Health’s Clinical AI Agent.

Oracle’s official customer page confirms AtlantiCare’s implementation of
Oracle Health AI Agents, but the page reviewed does not state the exact
26-specialty figure.

Therefore, the number can be used only with direct attribution to the CIO and
Becker’s article.

**Approved wording:**

> According to AtlantiCare CIO Jordan Ruch, as quoted by Becker’s Hospital
> Review in July 2025, approximately 260 providers across 26 specialties were
> using Oracle Health’s Clinical AI Agent.

**Do not claim:**

- Oracle’s official customer page reports 26 specialties.
- The figure represents AtlantiCare’s current 2026 deployment size.
- All 26 specialties achieved the same results.
- AtlantiCare uses Ambience Healthcare.

**Sources:**

- [Becker’s: AtlantiCare cuts documentation time with Oracle AI](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ehrs/atlanticare-cuts-documentation-time-by-50-with-oracle-ai/)
- [Oracle customer story: AtlantiCare](https://www.oracle.com/customers/atlanticare/)

## Claims removed from the portfolio narrative

The following claims are cut unless stronger evidence is later found:

1. “Jefferson is an Ambience customer.”
2. “Jefferson plans to replace Abridge.”
3. “Ambience produces Houston Methodist’s nursing notes.”
4. “Oracle’s official page reports AtlantiCare’s 26-specialty scope.”
5. “P4 represents a real healthcare account.”
6. Any suggestion that the synthetic grades measure results at these
   organizations.

## Interview explanation

> I researched public information to make the hypothetical plays realistic,
> but I maintained a claim-verification document so that public facts,
> attributed reports, and my own hypotheses would not be mixed together.
> Jefferson’s Abridge use and Houston Methodist’s Ambience hospital program
> were supported by organizational sources. The specific vendor behind Houston
> Methodist’s nursing pilot was not identified, so I labeled that detail
> unverified. AtlantiCare’s 26-specialty figure came from a direct CIO quote in
> Becker’s, so I retained it with clear attribution. Every synthetic outcome
> remains separate from those public facts.
